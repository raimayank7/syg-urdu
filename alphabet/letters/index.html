The Urdu alphabet, letter by letter

The Urdu alphabet is written from right to left in a flowing script that came to India through Persian. Its base is the Arabic alphabet of 28 letters. Persian added four letters for sounds Arabic lacks (پ چ ژ گ), and Urdu added three more for the retroflex sounds of India (ٹ ڈ ڑ). Lists of the alphabet give between 36 and 39 letters, depending on whether the nasal ں, the aspirating ھ, the hamza and the two forms of ye are counted separately. The table below shows them all.

For a reader of Hindi the task is lighter than it looks, for two reasons. First, you already know the language; you are only learning a second way to write it. Second, the letters come in families that share one basic shape and differ only in their dots. ب پ ت ٹ ث are one boat-like shape with one dot below, three dots below, two dots above, a small ط above, and three dots above. Learn seven or eight shapes and the dots, and you have most of the alphabet.

The Devanagari column shows how each letter is normally written when Urdu words appear in Hindi. Where several Urdu letters share one sound, the same Devanagari letter appears more than once.

Letters that share a sound

Arabic has several sounds that Urdu speakers do not distinguish, so Urdu ends up with more than one letter for the same sound. The spelling of each word must simply be learnt, as English spellers learn "c", "k" and "ck".

A useful rule: the rarer letters (ث ح ذ ص ض ط ظ ع ق) appear almost only in words borrowed from Arabic. Native Indian words such as पानी, घर, रोटी never contain them. More on this in letters with the same sound.

Five sounds worth practising

  • خ (kh): a rough sound from the back of the throat, as in ख़त, ख़बर. It is not the Hindi ख.
  • غ (gh): the voiced partner of the above, like a gentle gargle: ग़म, ग़लत.
  • ق (q): a k made deep in the throat: क़लम, वक़्त.
  • ز (z) and ف (f): ज़मीन, फ़िक्र, not "jameen" or "phikr".

These are the same five sounds Hindi marks with a dot below the letter. See the five nuqta sounds.

The breathy sounds

Urdu has no separate letters for भ, फ, थ, ठ, झ, छ, ध, ढ, ख, घ. Each is written as the plain letter followed by the "two-eyed h", ھ: بھ پھ تھ ٹھ جھ چھ دھ ڈھ کھ گھ. Read the pair as one sound. گھر is ghar, not "g-har".
