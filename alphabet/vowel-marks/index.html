Urdu vowels and marks

Here lies the biggest difference between writing Hindi and writing Urdu. In Devanagari every vowel is written; nothing is left to guess. In Urdu, as in Arabic and Persian, short vowels are normally not written at all. The word کل is just k-l. The reader supplies the vowel: "kal".

Marks for the short vowels do exist. They are small strokes placed above or below a letter, called aeraab. Children's primers, dictionaries and the Quran print them. Newspapers, novels and signboards leave them out, and fluent readers do not miss them, just as you can read "bldg" or "pls". You know the language, so you already know which vowel a word needs. A learner who does not know Hindi-Urdu finds this hard. For you it is a small hurdle.

Long vowels are a different matter. They are written with three of the alphabet's own letters, ا و ی, and they are always there.

One letter, several jobs

Notice in the table that و can be the consonant w (وقت waqt) or the vowels oo, o and au. ی can be the consonant y (یاد yaad) or the vowel ee. At the start of a word they are consonants; after a consonant they are usually vowels. Which vowel exactly, oo or o, is again supplied by your knowledge of the word. دو could in theory be "doo", but you know the word is दो.

Words that begin with a vowel

A word cannot begin with a bare vowel mark, so a carrier letter is used. Short a, i and u at the start are written with ا: اب ab, اِس is, اُس us. Long aa is آ. Other long vowels put ا in front of the vowel letter: ایک ek, اور aur. In Arabic words the letter ع often plays the same carrier role: عمر umr, عید Eid.

How to read without the marks

  1. Read the consonants and long vowels from right to left: کتاب gives k-t-aa-b.
  2. Try the short vowels that make a word you know: kitaab. Your vocabulary does the work.
  3. Use the sentence. اس may be "is" (this) or "us" (that); the context decides, exactly as "read" in English may be present or past.
  4. Learn the fifty commonest words as shapes. ہے، میں، کا، کی، کے، کو، سے، پر، اور، نہیں make up a large share of any page. Once these are instant, the rest of the line falls into place.

Same spelling, different words

Because short vowels are unwritten, a few pairs look identical: میں is both "main" (I) and "mein" (in); اس is "is" and "us"; ان is "in" and "un". Careful writers add a zer or a pesh when confusion is possible. Mostly nobody bothers, and nobody is confused.
