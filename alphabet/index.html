About the Urdu script

Urdu is written in a script that descends from the Arabic alphabet by way of Persian. It runs from right to left, its letters join to one another inside a word, and it is traditionally written in the graceful, sloping style known as Nastaliq. To the 28 letters of Arabic, Persian added four and Urdu a few more, so that every sound of the language of North India can be written, including the retroflex ट, ड, ड़ and all the breathy consonants.

For someone who reads Hindi, learning this script is not learning a language. It is learning a second dress for a language you already wear. The sentences are the same; "आप कैसे हैं?" simply becomes آپ کیسے ہیں؟

Three things that are new

  1. Direction. You read and write from the right. Numbers, however, run from the left.
  2. Joining. Letters change shape depending on their place in the word. Most keep their dots and lose only their tails.
  3. Unwritten short vowels. The little a, i and u are usually left out. Because you know the words, you supply them without effort.

How to use these lessons

Go in order: the letters, then joining, then vowels and marks. The table of letters gives each one's name, its sound, the Devanagari letter normally used for it and a familiar word. After the three lessons, read the everyday words with the helper lines covered, and test yourself on the practice page. A day-by-day route is laid out in learn the script in ten days.

The Urdu on this site is shown in a Nastaliq font where your device can load one. If it appears in a flatter style, your device is using a fallback font; the letters are the same.
