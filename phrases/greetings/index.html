Greetings

15 phrases in Urdu, each in the script with a roman reading, the Hindi meaning and the English meaning. Tap Listen to hear it, where your device has a Urdu voice.

Urdu is famous for its courtesy, and that courtesy begins with the greeting. Two greetings cover almost every meeting. السلام علیکم (assalamu alaikum), "peace be upon you", is the usual greeting among Muslims and is widely used by Urdu speakers in general; the reply is وعلیکم السلام (wa alaikum assalam), "and upon you be peace". آداب (aadaab), from the word for respect and good manners, is the graceful all-purpose greeting of the Urdu-speaking culture of North India and the Deccan, used between people of every faith. It is often said with the right hand raised towards the forehead.

If you speak Hindi, you already understand most of this page. "आप कैसे हैं?" is the same sentence in both languages. What changes is the script, and a layer of Persian and Arabic words that give Urdu its flavour: ख़ुश आमदीद for welcome, ख़ुदा हाफ़िज़ for goodbye, शब बख़ैर for good night.

  • السلام علیکم

    assalamu alaikum

    अस्सलामु अलैकुम (आप पर सलामती हो)

    Peace be upon you (hello)

  • وعلیکم السلام

    wa alaikum assalam

    वअलैकुम अस्सलाम (जवाब में)

    And upon you be peace (the reply)

  • آداب

    aadaab

    आदाब (नमस्कार)

    Greetings (a polite hello)

  • خوش آمدید

    khush aamdeed

    ख़ुश आमदीद (स्वागत है)

    Welcome

  • آپ کیسے ہیں؟

    aap kaise hain?

    आप कैसे हैं?

    How are you? (to a man)

  • آپ کیسی ہیں؟

    aap kaisi hain?

    आप कैसी हैं?

    How are you? (to a woman)

  • میں ٹھیک ہوں، شکریہ

    main theek hoon, shukriya

    मैं ठीक हूँ, शुक्रिया

    I am fine, thank you

  • کیا حال ہے؟

    kya haal hai?

    क्या हाल है?

    How are things?

  • سب خیریت ہے؟

    sab khairiyat hai?

    सब ख़ैरियत है? (सब कुशल है?)

    Is all well?

  • آپ سے مل کر خوشی ہوئی

    aap se mil kar khushi hui

    आपसे मिलकर ख़ुशी हुई

    Pleased to meet you

  • صبح بخیر

    subah bakhair

    सुबह बख़ैर (सुप्रभात)

    Good morning

  • شب بخیر

    shab bakhair

    शब बख़ैर (शुभ रात्रि)

    Good night

  • خدا حافظ

    khuda hafiz

    ख़ुदा हाफ़िज़ (अलविदा)

    Goodbye (may God protect you)

  • پھر ملیں گے

    phir milenge

    फिर मिलेंगे

    We will meet again

  • اپنا خیال رکھیے گا

    apna khayal rakhiyega

    अपना ख़याल रखिएगा

    Please take care of yourself

Notes

  • بخیر (bakhair) means "with well-being". It joins to times of day: صبح بخیر morning, شام بخیر evening, شب بخیر night. In daily life, though, people mostly use salam or aadaab at any hour.
  • خدا حافظ and اللہ حافظ (Allah hafiz) both mean "God keep you" and are both in common use as goodbye.
  • The verb shows the speaker's gender, as in Hindi: a man says میں ٹھیک ہوں either way, but "I have come" is آیا ہوں for a man and آئی ہوں for a woman.
  • The Urdu question mark is turned the other way: ؟. The full stop is a short dash: ۔
