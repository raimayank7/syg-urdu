Food and home

15 phrases in Urdu, each in the script with a roman reading, the Hindi meaning and the English meaning. Tap Listen to hear it, where your device has a Urdu voice.

Hospitality sits at the heart of Urdu-speaking homes, and the language of the table is rich. The cloth on which a meal is laid is the دسترخوان (dastarkhwan). Food that tastes good is لذیذ (lazeez). And a host does not say "eat"; a host says نوش فرمائیے (nosh farmaiye), a courtly phrase for "please partake".

Most sentences on this page are plain Hindustani that you already speak; reading them in the Urdu script is the exercise. Look out for letters that have no single Devanagari twin. ذ and ز in لذیذ and زیادہ are both said "z", the sound Devanagari writes with a dot: ज़. خ in دسترخوان is the throaty ख़.

One spelling here surprises every learner: خوان is written with a و (w) that is not pronounced. The word is said "khaan", not "khwaan". Persian words beginning with خو often behave this way, as in خواب (khaab, dream) and خواہش (khaahish, wish).

  • آپ نے بہت تکلّف کیا

    aap ne bahut takalluf kiya

    आपने बहुत तकल्लुफ़ किया (इतनी मेहमाननवाज़ी की ज़रूरत नहीं थी)

    You have taken too much trouble for us

Words of the table

روٹی roti, چاول chaawal (rice), دال daal, سبزی sabzi (vegetable), گوشت gosht (meat), دودھ doodh (milk), شکر shakar (sugar), میٹھا meetha (sweet), ناشتہ naashta (breakfast). Many of these are the very words Hindi uses, because the kitchen is where the two languages have always been one.

تکلّف (takalluf) is formality or fuss. A guest says "तकल्लुफ़ मत कीजिए" to mean "please do not go to any trouble", and a host who wants you to relax says "बेतकल्लुफ़ रहिए".
