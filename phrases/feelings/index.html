Feelings

15 phrases in Urdu, each in the script with a roman reading, the Hindi meaning and the English meaning. Tap Listen to hear it, where your device has a Urdu voice.

Urdu has a reputation as the language of feeling, and its emotional vocabulary is one reason Hindi films and songs lean on it so heavily. Words such as ख़ुशी, उदासी, फ़िक्र, फ़ख़्र, अफ़सोस, मोहब्बत are all Urdu words of Persian or Arabic origin that Hindi speakers use every day without a second thought.

The grammar of feeling is the same as in Hindi. Many feelings "come to" or "happen to" a person, so the person takes مجھے (mujhe, "to me") instead of "I": मुझे फ़िक्र है, "to me there is worry". Others use میں ... ہوں (main ... hoon), "I am": मैं ख़ुश हूँ.

Two words for love deserve a note. محبت (mohabbat) is love in the widest sense: for a person, a parent, a country. عشق (ishq) is intense, consuming love, the love of poetry. Both begin with letters that have no everyday Devanagari twin, م-ح with the "heavy" h and ع (ain), which you can read about on the letters page.

Praise in Urdu

Urdu has a whole register of praise, heard at every mushaira (poetry gathering) and now in everyday talk: واہ واہ (waah waah, bravo), بہت خوب (bahut khoob, very fine), کیا کہنے (kya kehne, what can one say!), سبحان اللہ (subhan Allah, glory be to God, said at something beautiful). To accept praise modestly, say ذرّہ نوازی ہے (zarra nawazi hai), "you are being kind to a mere speck".
