Polite words and tehzeeb

16 phrases in Urdu, each in the script with a roman reading, the Hindi meaning and the English meaning. Tap Listen to hear it, where your device has a Urdu voice.

Tehzeeb (تہذیب) means refinement and good manners, and Urdu is the language most closely tied to that idea in India. Its polite phrases have a warmth that plain requests lack. Where ordinary speech says "sit down", polished Urdu says تشریف رکھیے (tashreef rakhiye): "please place your honoured presence here".

The key word is تشریف (tashreef), "honour". A guest does not simply come; he तशरीफ़ लाता है, "brings his honour". He does not sit; he तशरीफ़ रखता है. The speaker lifts the listener up. For the speaker's own actions the humble word is used: you say "मैं हाज़िर हूँ" (I am present, at your service), never "I have brought my honour".

These expressions are still alive in Lucknow, Hyderabad, Delhi and in Urdu writing everywhere, and you hear them in older Hindi films. Use them with elders and on formal occasions. Among friends the simpler शुक्रिया and माफ़ कीजिए are enough.

Notes

  • جی (ji) alone is a respectful "yes?" or "pardon?", and added to any answer it softens it, exactly as in Hindi.
  • براہِ کرم is the formal "please" of notices and letters. In speech, politeness is carried by the verb ending -یے (-iye): दीजिए, बैठिए, सुनिए.
  • اجازت دیجیے is how a guest announces that he would like to leave. The host is expected to protest a little before agreeing.
  • The reply to مبارک ہو is خیر مبارک (khair mubarak), "blessings to you too".
