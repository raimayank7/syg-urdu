Market and travel

16 phrases in Urdu, each in the script with a roman reading, the Hindi meaning and the English meaning. Tap Listen to hear it, where your device has a Urdu voice.

In the bazaar and on the road you meet written Urdu most often: on shop boards, bus fronts, station signs and banknotes. Learning these sentences in script therefore pays off twice, once for speaking and once for reading the world around you in cities such as Lucknow, Hyderabad, Bhopal, old Delhi and Srinagar.

The vocabulary shows Urdu's Persian and Arabic layer clearly. Price is قیمت (qeemat), a receipt is رسید (raseed), fare is کرایہ (kiraya), a shop is دکان (dukaan), a road is راستہ (raasta). Hindi uses most of these words too, and writes them with a dot under the letter where the sound is not native: क़ीमत.

That first sound, ق (q), is made further back in the throat than an ordinary k. In relaxed speech many people say plain "k", but careful Urdu speakers keep the two apart, and it is the mark of good pronunciation.

Signs you can now read

دکان dukaan (shop), ہوٹل hotel, اسپتال aspataal (hospital), ڈاک خانہ daak khaana (post office), بینک bank, اسکول school, مسجد masjid (mosque), کتب خانہ kutub khaana (library, bookshop), داخلہ daakhila (entry), ممنوع mamnoo (forbidden).

English words beginning with "st" or "sk" get a helping vowel in front when written in Urdu, as they do in many North Indian mouths: اسٹیشن is written "isteshan", اسکول "iskool".
