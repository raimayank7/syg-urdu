Everyday words and verbs

26 words in Urdu, each in the script with a roman reading, the Hindi meaning and the English meaning. Tap Listen to hear it, where your device has a Urdu voice.

This page is for reading practice. Every word on it is one you already know from Hindi, so that all your attention can go to the letters. Cover the roman and Devanagari lines with your hand, read the Urdu from right to left, say the word, then uncover to check. Twenty minutes with this page is worth a week of staring at the alphabet chart.

A few reading hints before you begin. Short vowels are usually not written in Urdu, so گھر is just g-h-r, and you supply the "a": ghar. Because you know the word, your mind fills the gap, which is exactly how fluent readers read. Long vowels are written: ا for aa, و for oo or o, ی for ee.

The verbs are given in the dictionary form, which in Urdu as in Hindi ends in -نا (-na): آنا aana, جانا jaana. Once you can read that ending, you can spot a verb anywhere on a page.

What to notice

  • ڑ in سڑک and کھڑکی is the flapped ड़; with the two-eyed h it becomes ڑھ, ढ़, as in پڑھنا.
  • The final ں in گاؤں, an n without a dot, is not a consonant. It makes the vowel nasal, like the Devanagari ँ.
  • عورت begins with ع (ain), which here simply carries the vowel "au".
  • Make sentences: میں اردو پڑھنا سیکھ رہا ہوں (main Urdu padhna seekh raha hoon), "I am learning to read Urdu."
