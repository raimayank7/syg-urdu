Days and time

20 words in Urdu, each in the script with a roman reading, the Hindi meaning and the English meaning. Tap Listen to hear it, where your device has a Urdu voice.

Four of the seven Urdu day names are the same as in Hindi, and three are different, so this is a page where a Hindi speaker does have something to learn. Monday is پیر (peer) where Hindi says सोमवार. Thursday is جمعرات (jumerat), "the eve of Friday". Friday is جمعہ (juma), the day of congregation. Saturday is ہفتہ (hafta), and that same word also means "a week". Tuesday, Wednesday and Sunday are the familiar मंगल, बुध, इतवार.

Time words mix both sources too. आज, कल, परसों, सुबह, शाम, रात are shared by both languages. वक़्त (time), लम्हा (moment), रोज़ (day) and साल (year) come from Arabic and Persian. As in Hindi, کل (kal) means both yesterday and tomorrow, and the verb tells you which.

The months of the ordinary calendar are the English names written in Urdu letters: جنوری، فروری، مارچ (janwari, farwari, march).

Notes

  • To ask the time: کیا وقت ہوا ہے؟ (kya waqt hua hai?) or کتنے بجے ہیں؟ (kitne baje hain?).
  • Many words here end in a silent ہ: ہفتہ، مہینہ، گھنٹہ، جمعہ. That final letter is not said as "h". It stands for the vowel "a", so the words are read hafta, mahina, ghanta, juma.
  • صبح (subah) ends in the "heavy h" ح, which here is spoken. Compare the two h letters on the letters page.
