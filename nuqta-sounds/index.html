The five nuqta sounds: क़ ख़ ग़ ज़ फ़

When Urdu words are written in Devanagari, five letters get a small dot underneath: क़, ख़, ग़, ज़, फ़. The dot is called a nuqta (نقطہ, "point"). It tells the reader that the sound is not the ordinary Hindi one but a sound that came with Persian, Arabic and later English words. Getting these five right is the quickest way to make your Urdu, and your Hindi, sound polished.

With nuqtaUrdu letterSoundPlain letterExample
क़قq: a k from deep in the throatकक़लम, قلم
ख़خkh: a rough, scraping sound at the back of the mouthखख़बर, خبر
ग़غgh: the same, with voice; a soft gargleगग़लत, غلط
ज़ز ذ ض ظz: as in English "zoo"जज़मीन, زمین
फ़فf: upper teeth on the lower lipफफ़ोन, فون

How to make each sound

  • z (ज़). Say a long "sss". Now switch on your voice without moving your tongue: "zzz". That buzz is the sound. It is not ज, where the tongue first stops the air.
  • f (फ़). Rest your upper teeth on your lower lip and blow. For फ the two lips close and burst open; for फ़ they never close.
  • kh (ख़). Raise the back of your tongue as for "k" but do not close fully; let the air scrape through. It is the sound at the end of the Scottish word "loch".
  • gh (ग़). The same position as kh, with the voice switched on. It feels like the beginning of a gargle.
  • q (क़). A "k" made as far back as you can, where the tongue meets the soft part of the palate near the throat. It has a hollow, deep quality.

Common slips

  • Dropping the sound: "jindagi" for ज़िंदगी, "jaroor" for ज़रूर, "phikar" for फ़िक्र.
  • Adding it where it does not belong: "फ़िर" for फिर, "फ़ल" for फल, "मज़बूर" for मजबूर (مجبور, written with ج). The rule of thumb: the dotted sounds occur only in words of Persian, Arabic or English origin, never in native Hindi or Sanskrit words. फूल, फल, फिर, जल, जीवन have no dot.
  • English words. The f and z of English follow the same letters: फ़ोन, फ़ाइल, ऑफ़िस, ज़ीरो, ज़ू.

Is the dot compulsory in Hindi?

Usage varies. Many Hindi newspapers and writers keep the dot for ज़ and फ़ and drop it for the other three; some drop all five; careful publications keep all five. In speech, ज़ and फ़ are widely pronounced, while क़, ख़, ग़ are kept mainly by speakers with an Urdu background. In Urdu itself there is no choice: ق and ک are different letters, and using the wrong one is a spelling mistake.

Practice line

Read this aloud slowly, giving each dotted letter its full value: "ज़रा ग़ौर से देखिए: ख़ूबसूरत काग़ज़ पर क़लम से लिखा फ़ैसला।" In Urdu script: ذرا غور سے دیکھیے: خوبصورت کاغذ پر قلم سے لکھا فیصلہ۔
