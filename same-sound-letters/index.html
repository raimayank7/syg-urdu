Urdu spelling: which s, which z, which t, which h?

A learner who has mastered the Urdu letters soon meets a puzzle. سب، صبح، ثبوت all begin with the sound "s", yet each begins with a different letter. Why does Urdu need three letters for one sound, and how does anyone know which to use?

Where the extra letters come from

Arabic has sounds that Urdu, Persian and Hindi speakers do not make. In Arabic, س, ص and ث are three clearly different sounds. When Arabic words entered Persian and then Urdu, speakers pronounced all three as a plain "s", but kept the original spelling out of respect for the source, much as English keeps the "ph" of "philosophy" from Greek. The result is a set of letters that sound alike in Urdu.

zزذ ض ظ

a (vowel carrier)اع

The rule that settles most cases

Words that are native to India, and words from Persian or English, use the everyday letter. सब, साथ, सात, सड़क, सोना, स्कूल are all written with س. तुम, तीन, तारा take ت. हम, हाथ, हवा take ہ.

The special letters appear only in words of Arabic origin. So the question "which s?" really arises only for Arabic words, and those have to be learnt one by one. The lists below cover the commonest.

Common words with each special letter

ص (suad): s

صبح subah (morning), صاف saaf (clean), صاحب sahib (sir), صحیح sahih (correct), صرف sirf (only), صبر sabr (patience), خاص khaas (special), صدی sadi (century).

ث (se): s

ثبوت suboot (proof), ثابت saabit (proved), اثر asar (effect), مثال misaal (example), اکثر aksar (often), ثواب sawaab (merit).

ذ (zaal): z

ذرا zara (a little), ذکر zikr (mention), ذمہ zimma (responsibility), لذیذ lazeez (tasty), کاغذ kaaghaz (paper), ذہن zehan (mind).

ض (zuad): z

ضرور zaroor (surely), ضرورت zaroorat (need), ضد zid (stubbornness), مضمون mazmoon (essay), حاضر haazir (present), رمضان Ramzaan.

ظ (zoe): z

ظاہر zaahir (evident), ظلم zulm (cruelty), نظر nazar (sight), انتظار intezaar (waiting), حفاظت hifaazat (protection), لفظ lafz (word).

ط (toe): t

طرف taraf (side), طرح tarah (manner), طالب علم talib-e-ilm (student), خط khat (letter), غلط ghalat (wrong), طاقت taaqat (strength), مطلب matlab (meaning).

ح (badi he): h

حال haal (condition), حق haq (right), محبت mohabbat (love), صبح subah (morning), حکومت hukoomat (government), حساب hisaab (account), صحت sehat (health).

ع (ain)

عمر umr (age), علم ilm (knowledge), عزت izzat (honour), عادت aadat (habit), معلوم maaloom (known), شروع shuroo (beginning), عید Eid, دعا dua (prayer).

Three ways to remember

  1. Learn word families. Arabic builds many words from a root of three consonants, and the root keeps its letters. Once you know علم (ilm, knowledge) has ع, you know that عالم (aalim, scholar), معلوم (maaloom, known), تعلیم (taaleem, education) and معلومات (information) have it too. حکم، حاکم، حکومت (order, ruler, government) share ح ک م.
  2. Read a lot. Spelling is learnt by the eye. A word seen thirty times is spelt correctly without effort.
  3. Keep a list. Write the Arabic-letter words you meet in a notebook, grouped by letter as on this page.

Does it matter for reading?

Hardly at all. When you read, every one of these letters simply gives its sound. The puzzle exists only when you write. So do not let it delay your reading; come back to this page when you begin to write Urdu yourself.
