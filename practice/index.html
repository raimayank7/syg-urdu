How the quiz works

In Letters mode you see one Urdu letter in its separate form and choose its sound from four options. In Words mode you see a whole word and choose its English meaning; the words come from our word lists and are joined up, as in real writing, so this mode tests true reading. After each answer the correct option turns green and the reading is shown. Your score lasts only while the page is open. Nothing is stored or sent.

Why some letters share an answer

Urdu has three letters that sound "s", four that sound "z", two that sound "t" and two that sound "h". The quiz will never offer you the same sound twice among the four options, so there is always exactly one right answer. If you are curious which words take which letter, read letters with the same sound.

How to practise well

  1. Begin with Letters until you can score twenty in a row. Watch the dots: they are what separate ب پ ت ث and ج چ ح خ.
  2. Move to Words as soon as you can. Sound each word out from right to left before you look at the options.
  3. Say the Devanagari twin aloud. Linking each Urdu letter to a Hindi letter you already know is the fastest route.
  4. Write down what you miss and copy each missed word five times.
  5. A few minutes, often. Three short rounds a day beat one long sitting.

After the quiz

Reading single words is a step, not the goal. When Words mode feels comfortable, go to the phrase pages, cover the helper lines and read whole sentences. Then find something real: a headline, a signboard, a line of poetry. That is where the script becomes yours.
