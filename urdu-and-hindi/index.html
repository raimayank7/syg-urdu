Urdu and Hindi: how alike, how different

Ask a linguist whether Hindi and Urdu are one language or two, and the usual answer is: one spoken language with two literary forms. The everyday speech of a Hindi speaker and an Urdu speaker in the same town is practically the same. The differences lie in the script, in the higher vocabulary, and in the literary and cultural traditions each has gathered. This page sets them out plainly.

What is the same

  • Grammar. The verb forms, the tenses, the postpositions (का, की, के, को, से, में, पर, ने), the genders and the word order are shared. "मैं कल बाज़ार गया था" is a correct sentence in both.
  • Core vocabulary. Pronouns, numbers, family words, body parts, common verbs and everyday nouns such as पानी, रोटी, घर, हाथ, आना, जाना are the same.
  • Sounds. Both use the retroflex and the breathy consonants of India. Educated Hindi speech also uses the five borrowed sounds q, kh, gh, z, f.

The shared base grew up in and around Delhi and is often called Hindustani or Khari Boli.

The gap widens as the subject becomes more formal. A chat about dinner is the same in both. A news bulletin differs noticeably. A page of philosophy in pure Hindi and one in pure Urdu may be hard going for a speaker of the other. Our word pairs page lists common examples.

Three small pieces of Persian grammar in Urdu

Along with words, Urdu took a few constructions from Persian. They appear mainly in formal writing and poetry, and they are easy to learn.

1. Izafat: the joining "e"

A short "e" between two words means "of", with the order reversed from Hindi: دردِ دل (dard-e-dil) is "दिल का दर्द". It also joins a noun to its adjective: وزیرِ اعظم (wazir-e-azam), "chief minister", that is, prime minister. It is written as a small zer under the last letter of the first word, or with a hamza or ے after a vowel, and is often not written at all. You have met it in film titles and in "शेर-ए-पंजाब", "जान-ए-मन".

2. "O" for "and"

Two words of Persian or Arabic origin can be joined with و, read "o": شب و روز (shab-o-roz), night and day; آب و ہوا (aab-o-hawa), "water and air", meaning climate.

3. Plurals from Arabic and Persian

Besides the ordinary Hindustani plurals, some borrowed words keep their original ones in formal Urdu: سوال sawaal, questions سوالات sawaalaat; خبر khabar, news items اخبار akhbaar (now simply "newspaper"); صاحب sahib, gentlemen صاحبان sahibaan.

Where Urdu is spoken and written

Urdu is one of the languages listed in the Eighth Schedule of the Constitution of India and is in official use in several states and union territories. It is the national language of Pakistan. In India it has deep roots in Delhi, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Telangana, Maharashtra, Karnataka and Jammu and Kashmir, and in the film and music world of Mumbai. The Deccan has its own long-established variety, Dakhni.

Why learn the Urdu script if you know Hindi?

  • To read a vast literature of poetry and prose in the form in which it was written, where spelling and wordplay are preserved.
  • To read signs, books and family papers in Urdu-speaking regions.
  • To pronounce correctly. The script shows whether a word has z or j, f or ph, q or k, which Devanagari without its dots hides.
  • Because it is a short journey: the language is already yours. Ten days will do it.

This page describes the two forms as they are used and taught. It takes no side in debates about their history and names. Statements about official status are general; for exact legal positions, consult the Constitution of India and the laws of the state concerned.
