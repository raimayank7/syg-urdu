Learn to read Urdu in ten days: a plan for Hindi speakers

If you speak Hindi, you already speak Urdu's grammar and most of its everyday words. What stands between you and an Urdu newspaper, a ghazal in its own letters or your grandfather's diary is only the script. With twenty minutes a day, ten days are enough to read slowly. Speed then comes with use.

The plan groups the letters by shape, not by alphabetical order, because letters of one family differ only in their dots.

The ten days

Five habits that make it stick

  1. Write as you learn. Reading alone is too passive for a new script. Write each new letter ten times, then each practice word five times, moving your hand from right to left.
  2. Say the Hindi letter aloud. When you write ب, say "ब". You are building a bridge between two scripts for one language, and the letter table gives the Devanagari twin of every letter.
  3. Read words you know. Your own name, your city, the names of film stars and cricketers, the words of a song you love. A word you can check is a word you learn.
  4. Keep the helper line covered. On every page of this site the roman and Devanagari lines sit below the Urdu. Cover them with a strip of paper and uncover only to check.
  5. A little every day. Skipping a day costs more than you expect in the first two weeks.

What will slow you down, and why it is normal

  • Missing short vowels. You will sound out k-t-b and wonder. Then you will see it: kitaab. This is the reading skill itself, and it grows quickly.
  • Nastaliq. Printed Urdu slopes and stacks. Letters look different from the neat shapes in a chart. Count dots and teeth.
  • Several letters for one sound. Reading is not affected; only spelling is. Leave spelling for later.
  • Gaps inside words. Non-joining letters break a word into pieces. With practice the eye learns the wider gap that marks a new word.

After day ten

Read something real for ten minutes each day: the headlines of an Urdu newspaper's website, a children's story, or the Urdu line on a banknote and a railway station board. Keep a notebook of new words, written in both scripts. Within two or three months of this, reading Urdu stops being decoding and becomes reading.

This plan teaches the script to people who already speak Hindi or Urdu. If you do not speak the language, you will need a course in the language itself alongside it.
